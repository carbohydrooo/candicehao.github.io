The Question

How does disposable income affect the quality of mental-health services provided to a community?
01

Data

NHS mental-health service and patient-experience datasets.

02

Analysis

Excel, RStudio, two-sided t-tests, and Kruskal-Wallis testing.

Income-related differences appeared in some service indicators, while satisfaction stayed broadly stable over time.

Test correlation more directly and consider countries where mental-health care is paid out of pocket.

Story 03 / Independent Research

IB Internal Assessments · Biology & Chemistry

Two Questions

Two independent investigations—from forming a question and designing an experiment to statistical analysis, uncertainty, and formal scientific writing.

01 Biology · Acid Rain 02 Chemistry · Green Tea

Investigation 01 · Biology

Does acidity affect
seed germination?

Why this question?

Acid rain is a global environmental concern that can alter soil and water conditions. I wanted to investigate how increasing acidity might affect an essential early stage of plant development: seed germination.

Experimental setup

Simulated acid rain

  • Citric acid solutions from pH 3–7
  • 60+ mung-bean samples
  • Controlled hydroponic conditions
  • Biomass-change measurements

Statistical result

p < 0.001ANOVA showed significant differences between pH groups.

What surprised me

Significant—but unexpected

The pattern did not match my hypothesis or the trend described in prior literature. I reported that conflict rather than reshaping the conclusion.

What I would improve

A stronger follow-up

  • Increase the sample size within each group
  • Strengthen seed-quality controls
  • Design a follow-up around the unexpected pattern

Investigation 02 · Chemistry HL

Does brewing pH affect
antioxidants in green tea?

Why this question?

Tea drinking is part of my cultural background and everyday life. I had often heard that the pH of brewing water could influence tea’s health benefits, so I wanted to test whether water pH measurably affects its antioxidant content.

Experimental setup

Redox titration

  • Five brewing-water pH levels
  • KMnO₄ redox titration
  • Catechin concentration calculated
  • ~5% propagated uncertainty per trial

Observed result

≈ no effectWater pH had essentially no observable effect within the tested timeframe.

Looking beyond the result

The timescale mattered

Literature suggested catechin degradation at high pH occurs over hours—not within the roughly ten-minute brewing and titration window I tested.

The better next question

Extend the observation

How would catechin concentration change if tea brewed at different pH levels were measured repeatedly over several hours?

Eye Level Richmond · A teaching story

2 YearsFrom Tutor to
Tutor Trainer

Supporting young learners in English and Math—and learning how to adjust my teaching to the person in front of me.

Where
Eye Level Richmond
When
2023–2025
Subjects
English & Math
Students
Ages 6–14
Format
One-to-one & small groups

Adaptive lesson planning, made visible

My Teaching Approach

A lesson plan gave me a starting point, but each student’s questions, confidence, pace, and personality determined how I taught the material.
  1. 01Observe

    Notice how the student approaches a question.

  2. 02Explain

    Introduce the idea in a clear, manageable way.

  3. 03Check

    Ask questions and look for genuine understanding.

  4. 04Adjust

    Change the explanation, pace, or level of support.

  5. 05Encourage

    Recognize progress and build confidence.

Teaching happened one learner at a time

One Desk,
Many Learning Styles

“I can do this one myself.” A difficult concept finally clicks A hesitant student begins answering confidently Parents notice improvement

How I adapted

  • 01Broke difficult concepts into smaller steps
  • 02Changed explanations when the first approach did not connect
  • 03Balanced encouragement with clear expectations
  • 04Adjusted pacing for different ages and confidence levels
  • 05Made lessons engaging without losing curriculum goals

Recognition I didn’t expect

Being Trusted
to Come Back

Positive feedback from parents showed me that my adaptations were helping students improve. Being invited back during university breaks—and eventually being trusted to train incoming tutors—made me feel that the patience and responsibility I brought to each lesson had been noticed.
Manager feedback“I don’t need to worry when you’re here—you get things done in an organized, efficient, and effective way.”

Looking back

The Part I’m Most Proud Of

The most fulfilling moments were often the smallest ones—a student answering independently, becoming less hesitant, or finally understanding something that had frustrated them.

I’m proud not only that I became a tutor trainer, but that the promotion reflected two years of smaller choices: being patient, preparing carefully, adapting when something was not working, and treating every student’s progress as something worth taking seriously.
